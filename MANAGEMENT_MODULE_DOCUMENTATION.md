# Management Module — Component Documentation
### Bukidnon Bus Company @BUKSU — Administrator Module

*Every claim below is traced to the actual frontend and backend implementation through direct source-code inspection, not inferred from page titles, labels, or the screenshot snapshot supplied as a reference. Where a figure, label, or behavior could not be confirmed, or where the implementation diverges from what the interface appears to promise, that is stated plainly rather than smoothed over.*

---

## A. Overview of the Management Module

The Management module is the administrator's central record-keeping hub, organized into nine sections reachable from a single overview screen: Passenger, Bus, Bus Attendant, Driver, Location, Route, Schedule, Fare, and Admin Management. Each section manages a distinct category of operational record — ticketing activity, the vehicle fleet, on-board personnel, terminals and stops, corridor routes, dispatch timetables, fare pricing, and administrator accounts, respectively — and together they hold the reference data that the rest of the system depends on: a bus cannot be dispatched without a registered route, a route cannot be priced without configured terminals, and an attendant cannot issue a ticket without an assigned bus. The module gives administrators a consistent place to register, review, update, and retire these records as the company's operations grow, with each section's record count also summarized on the overview screen for a quick status check before drilling into any one area.

## B. Explanation of Each Component

**Management Overview.**
The Management Overview presents all nine sections as individual summary cards, each showing a headline figure (for example, the number of tickets issued, buses registered, or administrators on record), a short supporting figure for context, and a caption reading "Live counts · RBAC-protected." This caption is accurate in one respect and should not be over-read in another: the figures shown are genuinely retrieved fresh from the system each time the page loads rather than being fixed placeholder numbers, and the overview page itself is only reachable by a signed-in administrator — but the phrase should not be understood to mean that different administrator roles see different numbers on these cards. Every authenticated administrator who opens this page, regardless of their assigned role, is shown the exact same set of figures; the caption refers to the general principle that data behind the Management module requires authentication, not to the specific cards varying by role.

**Passenger Management.**
Despite its name, this section does not manage individual passenger accounts or profiles — the system has no concept of a registered passenger account at all. What it actually presents is the complete record of tickets issued across the fleet, functioning as an operational ticketing log rather than a passenger directory. The headline count shown both on the overview card and at the top of this page — currently sixteen — is the total number of tickets ever issued, the same figure in both places, and it should be read as a count of ticket transactions rather than a count of unique travelers, since the system does not currently track whether two tickets belong to the same returning passenger.

**Passenger Summary Cards.**
Above the ticket table, two summary figures are shown: the all-time total of tickets served, and a second figure reflecting however many records currently match whatever search or date filter the administrator has applied, together with the combined fare value of just those filtered records. The all-time figure stays fixed regardless of filtering; the second figure and its fare total update immediately as the administrator narrows or widens the current search.

**Passenger Search and Filters.**
Administrators can search tickets by typing part of a passenger identifier, and can further narrow the list by a date range — a specific day, month, or year, or a manually chosen start and end date — as well as by a small set of quick filter buttons for the company's main terminal hubs. There is no option to sort the list by a chosen column; records are always ordered with the most recently issued ticket first. There is also no page-by-page navigation control — the table loads and displays every matching record at once, up to an internal cap of three thousand records, which is well beyond the system's current ticket volume and is unlikely to be noticed in practice at this stage of operation.

**Passenger Records Table.**
Each row in the table shows the passenger identifier recorded at the time of issuance, the boarding and destination locations, the attendant and bus that issued the ticket, and the fare charged. An administrator can open an edit window directly from a row to correct the boarding location, destination, or fare amount, or can remove a ticket entirely after confirming the action in a warning prompt. Correcting or removing a ticket in this way only affects that one record — it does not retroactively change any bus's seat-occupancy count or any other calculation elsewhere in the system, since those are computed independently at the time they are needed rather than stored and kept in sync with edits made here.

**Ticket History.**
There is no separate, per-passenger ticket history view, because — as noted above — the system does not track a stable passenger identity across multiple tickets. What functions as "ticket history" is simply the same searchable table described above, filtered by typing a specific passenger identifier into the search field; the result is every ticket that happened to use that exact identifier, not a verified history belonging to one confirmed individual.

**Bus Management.**
This section is the registry of every bus in the company's fleet. Each bus is listed with its bus number, plate number, a general condition indicator, a partially hidden hardware identifier for its tracking device, its currently assigned attendant, and its assigned route. The overview card distinguishes between the total number of buses ever registered and the number currently marked "Active" — in the sixteen-ticket snapshot referenced for this documentation, these happen to be the same single bus. It is important to note precisely what "Active" means here: it is a status an administrator sets deliberately, through a dedicated activate or deactivate action on a bus's own detail page, each confirmed with a warning prompt before it takes effect. It is not something the system changes automatically based on whether that bus is currently transmitting a live GPS signal — a bus can be marked Active in this registry while its GPS has gone stale, and the two should not be assumed to mean the same thing. The bus's live signal freshness is instead shown separately, on that bus's own detail page rather than in this list.

**Bus Summary Cards.**
The two figures shown — total registered buses and currently Active buses — are described above; no further summary breakdown (such as buses under maintenance) is shown at the summary-card level, though the full status is visible per bus in the registry list.

**Bus Records and Forms.**
Registering a new bus requires a bus number, a fifteen-digit hardware tracking identifier, an assigned driver and attendant, an assigned route, and a seat capacity between one and three hundred; the form rejects the submission with a specific message if any of these conditions is not met. Once a bus is registered, its bus number and hardware identifier can no longer be changed — editing a bus is limited to reassigning its driver, attendant, or route, updating its plate number, or adjusting its seat capacity. Removing a bus from the registry requires the administrator to type the bus's exact number into a confirmation prompt before the delete button becomes active, and doing so permanently removes the bus record and its cached location history while explicitly leaving that bus's past ticket records untouched.

**Bus Attendant Management.**
This section lists the company's bus attendants, showing each one's name, email, phone number, a six-digit personnel identifier, and whether their access to the system has been deactivated. The overview card's attendant count is drawn from whichever of two internally tracked attendant figures is currently larger, since the system maintains attendant information in two related but not fully merged places; the list on this page itself is built by combining both of those sources into one view, so an attendant could appear here even if they are not reflected in one of the two individual counts. The "assigned to active buses" figure shown alongside the overview card is a separate calculation — a count of how many currently Active buses have an attendant linked to them — and it is only shown at the overview level, not repeated within this page itself.

**Attendant Records and Onboarding.**
Adding a new attendant is a guided, multi-step process carried out by the administrator on the attendant's behalf: an email is verified with a one-time code, then the attendant's name and a sign-in password of at least eight characters are set, which becomes the credential that attendant later uses to log into the Bus Attendant mobile app. This is an administrator-driven onboarding flow rather than a public self-registration page. Editing an existing attendant's record is limited to their name, middle name, and phone number. Removing an attendant is deliberately restricted: the system refuses the deletion outright, with an explanatory message, if that attendant has ever issued a ticket, requiring the administrator to reassign or otherwise resolve those records first — a genuine safeguard against silently orphaning ticket history, not merely a confirmation prompt. A separate access toggle, available from an attendant's individual detail page, lets an administrator suspend or restore an attendant's ability to log in without deleting their record outright, and this specific action is the one place in the entire Management module confirmed to leave a permanent, reviewable entry in the system's administrative audit trail.

**Driver Management.**
This section lists registered drivers, showing each one's name, contact details, a driver identifier, license number, and years of driving experience. A verification badge appears on a driver's individual detail page, though not in the main list. It is important to describe precisely what this verification represents: it confirms that an email address was successfully confirmed through a one-time code during an administrator-run onboarding process, the same style of flow used for attendants. It does not represent a license validity check or any confirmation of legal driving qualification — the license number itself is free-text information supplied during onboarding, with no cross-check against any external licensing authority built into the system. Separately, a driver's "active" status (whether they still appear on the roster at all) and their "verified" status (whether they completed the OTP onboarding step) are independent from one another; because the system also allows a driver record to be created through a simpler path that skips the OTP step entirely, a driver can be active on the roster without being verified, though a verified driver is always also active.

**Driver Records and Forms.**
The same guided, administrator-run onboarding pattern used for attendants applies to drivers — email verification, followed by name, license number, and years of experience, each validated before submission. Editing a driver's record revalidates the same required fields and checks that an updated email address is not already in use by another driver. Removing a driver does not erase their record outright; it marks the driver inactive and clears their email address so that address becomes available again for a future registration, meaning the underlying record is retained rather than permanently deleted.

**Location Management.**
This section manages the terminals and stops that the rest of the system's routing, scheduling, and fare calculations depend on. Unlike most of the other Management pages, Location Management includes its own interactive map directly on the page, on which an administrator can place a new terminal by clicking a location, drag an existing terminal's marker to adjust its position, and see a geofence boundary circle drawn around each registered point — the same style of boundary the system uses elsewhere to detect when a bus has arrived at a stop. The overview card's count of four hubs refers specifically to registered terminal records; any intermediate bus stops an administrator attaches to a given terminal are stored as part of that terminal's own record rather than counted as separate entries, so this figure should be read as "registered terminal locations," not as a combined count of every individual stop in the network. A location's relationship to the routes that use it is visible from the Route Management side — when building or editing a route, an administrator chooses its origin, destination, and any via-stops from the list of registered locations — but the reverse view (which routes currently use a given location) is not shown from within Location Management itself.

**Location Records and Forms.**
Registering or editing a location requires a name and a valid pair of coordinates, and the form prevents saving a location under a name that duplicates one already registered. Deleting a location requires a confirmation prompt but is not otherwise restricted by administrator role — any signed-in administrator can remove a registered location. A separate, read-only audit tool on this page allows an administrator to flag previously registered coordinates that appear to fall off any real road, for manual review, without making any automatic changes to the underlying records.

**Route Management.**
This section manages the corridor routes that connect the company's terminals — each entry showing its origin, destination, and any intermediate stops along the way. The overview card's headline route count is a combined figure: it adds together the number of corridor routes with the number of configured fare routes, rather than simply repeating the corridor count under a different label, so the two can diverge from one another if a corridor exists without a fully configured matching fare entry, or vice versa. In the reference snapshot used for this documentation, that combined figure happened to equal the corridor count alone because the fare-route figure was zero at that moment — worth noting if the same situation is observed again, since under normal operation each corridor route is expected to generate a matching fare-route entry automatically when it is created, and a lasting mismatch between the two is worth investigating directly rather than assumed to be routine. Deleting a corridor route is the one destructive action across the entire Management module that is genuinely restricted by administrator role in the interface itself: the delete control simply does not appear for an administrator who does not hold the highest (super-administrator) privilege level, rather than merely being grayed out or failing silently after the fact. Suspending or resuming a route, by contrast, is available to any signed-in administrator from that route's own detail page.

**Route Records and Forms.**
Creating a new route requires selecting a distinct origin and destination from the registered locations, with the save action unavailable until at least two locations exist in the system to choose from. The route list itself does not display a suspended/active badge or a fare-configuration indicator at a glance — those details are reserved for the route's individual detail page.

**Schedule Management.**
This section manages the dispatch timetable — the planned departure for each bus on a given service day. Each entry in the list shows the route, the departure point, the service date if one is set, the assigned bus, and a status such as on-time, delayed, or arriving. The dispatch-row figure shown on the overview card is a more specific count than the list itself displays: it reflects only the non-cancelled dispatch entries scheduled specifically for the current day in Philippine local time, while the Schedule Management page's own list shows every dispatch entry regardless of date, deduplicated to one entry per bus. Each entry's service date rolls forward to the current Philippine calendar day automatically the next time the dispatch data is loaded by anyone using the system — an administrator does not need to perform a manual daily action simply for the date itself to update, though a dedicated "publish to live board" action does exist for explicitly assigning a bus's departure for the current day. A bulk weekly-schedule generator and a holiday-override mechanism exist within the system but are not currently reachable from anywhere in this page's interface — they are not available actions for an administrator using this screen today. A separate, read-only "Live fleet departures" board, styled like an airport arrivals/departures display, presents the same dispatch information in a non-editable form; this should not be confused with the live bus-position map found on the View Location page, which is a different data feed entirely (vehicle GPS position, rather than planned departure times).

**Schedule Records and Forms.**
Creating a dispatch entry requires a bus, a route, and a scheduled departure time; the publish action specifically requires that the selected bus's assigned route resolve to a currently active (non-suspended) corridor, refusing the action with an explanatory message otherwise. An administrator can adjust a dispatch entry's departure time or remove it outright from the list, each a straightforward action without an additional confirmation step for editing, though removal is confirmed before it takes effect.

**Fare Management.**
This section manages how much a ticket costs. It is organized into two distinct parts: a set of global fare settings — a per-kilometer rate and discount percentages for student, person-with-disability, and senior-citizen passengers — and a separate list of individually priced terminal-to-terminal pairs, referred to here as the fare matrix. The overview card's count of three fare rows refers specifically to entries in this matrix — that is, three configured price rules between pairs of terminals — and this figure should not be mistaken for a count of payments actually collected or tickets actually sold; nothing on this page records whether a fare was paid, only what a ticket between two given points is configured to cost. Editing either the global settings or the fare matrix is restricted in the interface to administrators holding the highest privilege level — the relevant input fields and save buttons are shown as read-only or disabled for any other administrator viewing this page. One caveat worth noting for completeness: while this restriction is consistently enforced for the global settings and for removing a fare entry, it is not yet mirrored with equal strictness on the underlying system for adding or editing an individual fare-matrix entry, meaning the interface's own restriction is currently the primary safeguard for those two specific actions rather than a second, independent check elsewhere in the system. A historical log of fare changes is maintained internally by the system but is not currently displayed anywhere within this page or elsewhere in the administrator interface.

**Admin Management.**
This section's actual behavior differs in an important way from what its name might suggest, and that difference is worth describing precisely. Rather than presenting a live, editable list of every administrator account and its assigned role, this page displays a short activity summary for two specific, fixed administrator profiles, each showing how many recent tracked actions — such as record additions, edits, deletions, broadcasts, and logins — are attributed to that profile based on the system's general activity log. It does not offer any control, anywhere in the interface, for changing which access role an existing administrator holds, even though the underlying system is capable of supporting such role assignments; that capability simply has no corresponding button or form in the current interface. A genuinely separate account-management screen does exist, reachable from this page, but it is scoped specifically to information-technology support accounts rather than administrator accounts broadly — from there, a new IT-support account can be created through an email-verification process, and existing ones can be viewed or deactivated. The overview card's count of three administrators is, specifically, the larger of two figures the system tracks separately for administrator access — one counting formal role-assignment records and the other counting portal accounts flagged with administrator access — and because nothing in the system guarantees these two figures always move together, they should be understood as closely related but not strictly interchangeable. Unlike most of the other Management pages, this one does show a clear loading indicator while its activity data is being retrieved, and a clear on-page error message with a retry option if that retrieval fails, rather than relying solely on a brief pop-up notice.

## C. Data Sources and Implementation Status

| Section | What the figures/records actually represent | Implementation status |
|---|---|---|
| Management Overview | Nine live-retrieved summary figures, identical for every administrator role | Fully implemented; "RBAC-protected" refers to requiring authentication generally, not per-role variation |
| Passenger Management | A flat, searchable log of issued tickets — not passenger accounts | Fully implemented; no sorting or page-by-page browsing; capped well above current volume |
| Bus Management | Fleet registry; "Active" is an administrator-set status, independent of live GPS freshness | Fully implemented; GPS freshness is shown only on a bus's own detail page, not in this list |
| Bus Attendant Management | Personnel roster, merged from two related internal sources | Fully implemented; account deactivation is the only action here with a permanent audit record |
| Driver Management | Personnel roster; "verified" confirms an email-OTP step only, not license validity | Fully implemented; "active" and "verified" are independent statuses |
| Location Management | Registered terminals (with their attached stops); includes its own interactive map | Fully implemented; no role restriction on edits or deletion |
| Route Management | Corridor routes; the headline count combines corridors and fare-routes rather than repeating one figure | Fully implemented; route deletion is the only role-restricted action across the whole module |
| Schedule Management | Dispatch timetable; automatically rolls to the current Philippine calendar day | Mostly implemented; bulk weekly/holiday scheduling exists in the system but has no interface here |
| Fare Management | Pricing rules only — never payment or revenue records | Fully implemented for viewing/editing; the edit restriction for one specific action is enforced in the interface but not yet mirrored elsewhere in the system |
| Admin Management | An activity summary for two fixed profiles, not a live administrator roster | Partially implemented as a true account-management screen; role re-assignment has no interface anywhere |

## D. Testing and Verification

- **Source-code inspection:** performed exhaustively for every section documented above, covering both the administrator-facing interface and its corresponding backend logic.
- **Automated tests:** none were run as part of producing this documentation.
- **Browser or live-application testing:** none was performed. Every statement above is a code-level finding rather than an observation of the deployed pages; the reference figures supplied (sixteen tickets, one bus, and so on) were used only to interpret what the code computes, not independently re-confirmed against the live site.
- **API-level testing:** none was performed as part of producing this documentation.

## E. Final Documentation Summary

Taken together, the nine sections of the Management module give the administrator a consistent, centralized way to maintain every category of record the Bukidnon Bus Company @BUKSU system depends on — from the terminals a route connects, to the buses and personnel assigned to operate it, to the fares a passenger is charged and the timetable a bus follows each day. The module's components are generally implemented consistently with what their summary cards and record lists appear to promise, with a small number of specific exceptions documented plainly above rather than glossed over: the Admin Management section functions as an activity log rather than a true account roster, meaningful access restriction by administrator role exists in only a few specific places (most notably route deletion) rather than uniformly across the module, and certain planned conveniences — a bulk weekly scheduler, a visible fare-change history — exist at the system level without yet having a corresponding control in the interface. None of these observations suggest the module fails to support day-to-day fleet administration; rather, they mark the precise, current boundary between what has been built and what remains for future development, which is itself useful information for a system of this kind to document honestly as part of its own evaluation.
