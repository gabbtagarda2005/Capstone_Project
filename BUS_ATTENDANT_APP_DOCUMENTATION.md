# Bus Attendant Mobile Application — Component Documentation
### Bukidnon Bus Company @BUKSU — Bus Attendant Module

*The descriptions below are based on direct inspection of the mobile application's source code and the backend routes it calls. They describe what the attendant sees, what each control does, and whether a feature genuinely works end to end or only appears to, written in plain language rather than technical terms.*

---

**Login Screen.**
The attendant signs in using an email address and password, which are checked against the company's own staff records rather than any third-party account. A "Continue with Google" option is also offered, and this was confirmed to be a genuinely working alternative sign-in method, not a placeholder button — tapping it opens the device's real Google account picker, and the account chosen is verified by the system before the attendant is let in; this only succeeds for an email address already registered as a bus attendant in the company's own records, since signing in with Google does not create a new staff account by itself. Two separate recovery links are also provided, and it is worth noting that these are two distinct, fully working processes rather than one link doing two things: "Forgot password?" walks the attendant through confirming their email, entering a one-time verification code sent to them, and then choosing a new password; "Forgot email?" instead asks for the attendant's six-digit personnel identification number and, if recognized, reveals the email address already on file for that staff member. Both are genuine recovery flows connected to the real staff records, not cosmetic links.

**Home Screen.**
After signing in, the attendant's main screen shows a summary of their current shift — today's number of tickets issued, today's revenue collected, today's passenger count, and the route pairing they have issued the most tickets for. It is important to state plainly what this summary does and does not represent: every figure shown is drawn only from tickets that this specific attendant has personally issued, not from the company's fleet as a whole, so it should be read as a personal shift summary rather than a company-wide total. A row of filter-style buttons above the summary — labeled Tickets, Revenue, Passengers, and Corridor — appears to let the attendant narrow down the information being shown, but in the current implementation these only change which single figure is highlighted on screen; they do not filter, recalculate, or request any different data. A separate SOS button is also present on this screen, and this one is fully functional: tapping it opens a confirmation screen requiring the attendant to choose a severity level (Normal, Medium, or Emergency) and optionally add a short note before sending, after which the attendant's current device location is captured and a genuine alert is sent to the system. If sent while the device has no working internet connection, the alert is not lost — it is held on the device and automatically sent as soon as a connection becomes available again.

**Tickets Screen.**
This is where the attendant issues a new ticket to a boarding passenger. Rather than typing a location by hand, the attendant first taps "From" or "To" and then selects a starting hub from a short row of cards, after which a list of that hub's actual registered terminal and stops opens for final selection. This list of locations is drawn from the company's own current location records whenever the device is online; if the device cannot reach the system at that moment, a fixed, built-in list of the same company hubs and stops is shown instead, specifically so that ticket issuing can continue even without a connection. Four passenger categories are offered — Regular, Student, Senior Citizen, and Person with Disability — and choosing one of the discounted categories genuinely changes the fare rather than simply relabeling it. The fare amount itself is, in ordinary use, automatically calculated and sent back by the system the moment both a starting point and destination are chosen, and the field showing it cannot normally be typed into by the attendant at all. The one exception is when the device is offline and no previously seen fare for that same route and category has been remembered on the device — only then does the fare field become editable, clearly marked as an offline entry that the system will later double-check and finalize once a connection returns. When the attendant places the ticket, a successful result shows a brief on-screen confirmation with an option to undo it or share the ticket's details through the device's normal share options; if there is no connection at that moment, the ticket is not lost but is instead held on the device and automatically sent once a connection returns, with built-in protection to ensure it is recorded only once even if the same ticket is retried after reconnecting. If the ticket is rejected for a genuine reason other than a lost connection, the attendant's entered details remain on screen so the information does not have to be re-entered, along with an explanation of what went wrong.

**Passengers Screen.**
Despite its name, this screen does not show a list of passenger accounts or passenger profiles — the system does not currently have passenger accounts of any kind. What it actually shows is a list of the individual tickets this attendant has issued, each one a one-time transaction rather than a record tied to a returning, identifiable person. The attendant can search this list by ticket number, route, or category, and can sort or filter it by newest or oldest, fare amount, category, or route. Correcting a mistake on an already-issued ticket is only possible once the attendant is actively signed in to an assigned bus with their location sync active on the Home screen — without that, the correction button for every ticket on this screen is visibly disabled. When correction is available, making a change requires the bus driver to personally enter a private six-digit authorization code on the device before the attendant is allowed to adjust the route, category, or fare of that specific ticket; an incorrect code is simply rejected with an on-screen message, and the correction can be cancelled at any point before saving. Once saved, a confirmation message appears and the ticket list updates to reflect the change; no history or log of what was changed is shown to the attendant afterward on this screen, though the system does keep its own internal record of the correction.

**Profile Screen.**
This screen displays the attendant's own name and role, along with the company's officially configured name, logo, and contact details — the same shared company information used consistently throughout the rest of the system, so a change made to the company's branding elsewhere is reflected here automatically. A separate button opens a short summary of the attendant's own account details, such as their registered email, phone number, and assigned bus number. A dark mode switch is also available here, and this preference is genuinely remembered on the attendant's device even after fully closing and reopening the application. The "Generate shift summary" button produces a genuine, downloadable document — specifically a real PDF file summarizing the attendant's staff details, trip times and distance traveled, tickets sold and cash collected, the specific checkpoints reached during the shift, and the current health of the attendant's location-tracking connection — which is then handed to the device's normal share options so it can be saved or sent. One limitation is worth noting here: the small "last generated" note shown directly under this button is only remembered for as long as the attendant stays on this screen during the current app session; it is not saved anywhere and will show as blank again the next time the attendant opens the app, even though the actual report was successfully created earlier. Signing out from this screen is fully functional: it stops the device's ongoing location tracking, ends the attendant's active tracking session with the system, and clears the attendant's saved login details from the device, returning them to the login screen.

**Tactical Tracking / Location Sync Screen.**
This is not a separate screen the attendant navigates to, but a full-screen prompt that appears directly over the Home screen whenever the attendant has been assigned to a specific bus but has not yet shared their device's location for that assignment. Tapping its single "Sync My Location" action begins sharing the attendant's live location with the system; once this succeeds, the prompt disappears on its own and the normal Home screen becomes visible underneath, which is itself the confirmation that the sync worked — there is no separate "success" message beyond this. If the device's location services are turned off, or the attendant has not granted the app permission to use location, the prompt instead shows a clear message explaining that location access must be turned on before continuing; however, the application does not provide a direct button to jump straight to the device's settings screen for this — the attendant must open their device's settings manually. If the attendant currently has no bus assignment at all, this entire prompt is skipped, since there is nothing yet to track.

**Notifications and Navigation.**
A notification bell is available at all times, and tapping it opens a single combined list bringing together company-wide announcements, weather advisories relevant to the attendant's area, and alerts about lost items reported by passengers — all genuinely delivered from the system rather than shown as placeholder entries, with the bell's indicator clearing once the list has been opened. Moving between the application's main sections is done through four bottom navigation tabs — Home, Tickets, Passengers, and Profile — and all four were confirmed to be fully built, working destinations with no "coming soon" or unfinished sections among them. One further note for completeness: an older ticket-lookup screen exists elsewhere in the application's code but is not reachable from this navigation at all and was found to rely on fixed placeholder data rather than real records; since it cannot currently be opened from anywhere in the app, it is not part of the attendant's actual working experience and is mentioned here only for the sake of a complete and honest account of the codebase.

---

## Documentation Summary

The Bus Attendant Mobile Application gives on-duty staff a complete, largely genuine workflow for signing in, issuing and correcting tickets, monitoring their own shift performance, staying reachable during emergencies, and producing a real shift report — with sensible offline handling built in for ticket issuing and emergency alerts so that a lost connection delays rather than loses an attendant's work. A smaller number of details are worth carrying forward honestly: the Home screen's "Quick Focus" buttons only highlight information rather than filtering it, the Passengers screen lists ticket transactions rather than passenger identities since the system has no passenger accounts, the shift-summary "last generated" note is not actually remembered between app sessions even though the report itself is real, and the location-permission prompt tells the attendant what to do but does not take them directly to their device's settings to do it.

## Testing and Verification

This documentation is based entirely on direct inspection of the mobile application's source code and the backend routes it communicates with, including deliberately checking whether buttons such as Google sign-in, the recovery links, the SOS alert, and the shift-summary report are genuinely connected to working functionality rather than only appearing to be. It has not been confirmed through actual use of the running application on a phone or in a browser, and no live sign-in, ticket issuance, correction, or report generation was performed as part of producing this documentation.
