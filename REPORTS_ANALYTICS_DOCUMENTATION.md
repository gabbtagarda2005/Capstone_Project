# Reports & Data Analytics — Component Documentation
### Bukidnon Bus Company @BUKSU — Administrator Module

*Every claim below is traced to the actual frontend and backend implementation through direct source-code inspection, not inferred from labels or appearance. Where a figure's calculation method, data source, or "AI" labeling does not match what the interface implies, that is stated plainly rather than smoothed over.*

---

**Reports & Data Analytics Overview.**
The Reports & Data Analytics module gives the administrator a consolidated view of the company's ticketing performance — how much has been earned, how many passengers have boarded, which times and locations are busiest, and how individual buses, attendants, and routes are performing relative to one another. It draws its figures from the same underlying ticket records used throughout the rest of the system, presenting them as summary totals, trend charts, and comparison indicators rather than as a raw transaction log, so that an administrator can assess overall operational health at a glance before drilling into a specific category.

**Date and Bus Filters.**
A filter bar at the top of the module lets the administrator narrow the figures shown by a start date, an end date, and a specific bus, with a reset control to clear all three at once. Changing any of these filters causes the entire page to refresh against the newly scoped data — there is a single shared data source behind every card, chart, and report-category tab on this page, so narrowing the filter by date or bus affects everything shown below it uniformly, rather than some sections staying fixed while others update. One practical limitation worth noting: the bus and route options offered in these filter dropdowns are captured once, from whichever buses and routes happen to appear in the first set of records loaded when the page opens, and are not refreshed again afterward — a bus or route that only starts appearing in the data after that point will not appear as a filter option until the page is reloaded.

**Route Filter.**
Alongside the bus filter, a route selector lets the administrator narrow the report to a single route. The available route options are not drawn from the company's formally registered route list maintained elsewhere in the system; instead, they are built from whichever routes actually appear in the ticket records already loaded for the page, meaning a registered route with no ticket activity yet would not appear as a filter choice. Selecting a route scopes the figures on the page the same way the date and bus filters do, through the same single shared data refresh.

**Total Revenue (Actual).**
This figure is the sum of the fare amount recorded on every ticket ever issued, covering the system's complete history rather than any particular filtered window. It should be understood specifically as revenue calculated from issued-ticket fare amounts — the figure the attendant's device computed and recorded at the moment a ticket was issued — rather than as a confirmed record of cash or electronic payment actually collected; the system does not currently record a separate payment-confirmation step distinct from ticket issuance itself.

**Today's Intake.**
This is the total fare revenue recorded for the current day, determined using the Philippines' local calendar day as the boundary for what counts as "today," consistent with how the rest of the system defines a day. Beside this figure, a percentage change indicator compares it against the previous day's recorded revenue — this is a genuine calculated comparison, derived by the interface itself from the two most recent days in the system's short-term daily history, not a static or decorative figure, though it has no meaningful baseline to compare against on a day when no revenue has yet been recorded for either day.

**Last 24-Hour Revenue Flow.**
This component is a small trend chart showing revenue recorded across each hour of the current day, drawn as a compact line rather than a full chart. It reflects the current day's hour-by-hour figures specifically — effectively a shape of today's earnings so far, hour by hour — rather than a continuously rolling window spanning into the previous calendar day.

**Total Tickets.**
This is the total count of tickets issued across the system's entire history, independent of any filter currently applied to the rest of the page's figures. It represents a count of ticket transactions, not a count of unique passengers, since the system does not track whether two tickets were purchased by the same returning individual.

**Today's Tickets.**
This is the number of tickets issued specifically within the current Philippine calendar day. As with the revenue comparison described above, a percentage-change indicator beside this figure is a genuine comparison against the previous day's ticket count, calculated by the interface from the two most recent days of recorded activity, not a fixed or illustrative value.

**Tomorrow Projection.**
This figure is an estimate of the following day's revenue, and it is important to describe exactly how it is produced, because the system actually uses more than one method depending on how much recent data is available. Under normal circumstances — whenever the system has at least some recent days of ticket activity to work from — the estimate is built by weighting the past several days of revenue, giving more importance to the most recent days, with a modest additional adjustment applied specifically when the projected day falls on a weekend, reflecting typically higher weekend ridership. Only when there is no usable recent history at all does the system fall back to a simpler method: taking the current day's revenue (or, if that is also unavailable, a short recent average) and applying a fixed assumed growth rate to it. In either case, this figure is a calculated estimate based on recent historical patterns and simple assumptions — it is not produced by a trained predictive model or any external forecasting service, and it should not be presented or relied upon as a guaranteed future figure.

**Monthly Profit Goal.**
Despite its name, this component measures revenue progress, not profit in the accounting sense — the system does not track operating costs, expenses, or any other figure that would need to be subtracted from revenue to arrive at an actual profit. The component compares the current month's revenue, accumulated so far this month, against a fixed monthly target amount built into the system; the percentage shown reflects how far the month-to-date figure has progressed toward that target. The target amount itself is a fixed internal value rather than something an administrator can currently adjust through the interface.

**AI Forecast and Recommendation.**
Below the goal comparison, a short written line offers a month-end outlook and a specific operational suggestion. This outlook is calculated separately from the "Tomorrow Projection" figure described above — it takes the revenue accumulated so far in the current month and extends that pace in a straight line through the rest of the month's remaining days, then compares the resulting estimate against the fixed monthly target to describe how far ahead of or behind pace the month is tracking. The accompanying recommendation — suggesting additional service frequency along a named corridor during a particular time window — is generated from the same underlying logic described under "AI Insight" below: the system identifies whichever half of the day (morning or evening) has recently shown more boarding activity and attaches one of two fixed corridor-name phrases associated with that half of the day, rather than analyzing the named corridor's own ridership specifically. Although this feature and a related one elsewhere on the page are labeled "AI" in the interface, neither calls any external artificial-intelligence or machine-learning service; both are built from straightforward, deterministic arithmetic and pre-written phrasing. This should be read as a clearly labeled estimate and suggestion for the administrator's consideration, not as a verified forecast or an automated decision.

**Report Categories.**
Four report categories — Passenger, Bus Attendants, Bus, and Route — are presented as tabs, all drawing from the same already-loaded set of figures rather than each fetching its own separate data. The four are not built identically to one another. The Passenger tab offers a switchable view (by hour, day, month, or year) of both ticket volume and revenue, each with the system's identified peak period highlighted on the chart. The Bus Attendants tab, by contrast, has no such switchable time view at all — it always shows an all-time comparison of revenue and ticket activity across attendants, presented as a chart alongside a simple ranked leaderboard. The Bus tab combines a switchable ticket-volume chart with a revenue breakdown that can be filtered down to a single bus or shown as a proportional breakdown across the top-performing buses. The Route tab offers a switchable revenue-only chart (ticket volume is not broken out separately here) alongside a proportional breakdown of revenue share across the top routes, without a single-route drill-down option. Each tab additionally includes a sidebar summarizing the specific figures most relevant to that category.

**Export Data.**
An export option lets the administrator download the compiled report data as a PDF, a spreadsheet file, or a plain data file, with the option to choose which specific categories of information to include — for example, passenger activity, attendant performance, bus performance, route performance, the system's generated insights, or the underlying revenue series. This export's date range is set independently, within the export tool itself, rather than automatically following whatever date range is currently set in the page's main filter bar at the top — the two date controls are separate and are not kept in sync with one another. Additionally, the export does not appear to offer the same bus or route narrowing available in the main filter bar; it is scoped by date range and by which report categories are selected, not by a specific bus or route. If the system's primary export process is unavailable for any reason, a fallback path produces the same file formats directly from whatever report data is currently loaded and visible on the page at that moment — meaning that in this fallback scenario, the export does reflect whatever date, bus, and route filters happen to be active on the page at the time.

**Passenger Peak Hours.**
This chart identifies when passenger boarding activity is busiest, with a separate control letting the administrator switch between an hourly view of the current day, a daily view of the current month, a monthly view of the current year, or a full yearly view. On the hourly view specifically, the chart visually highlights the system's identified typical peak window — by default the morning hours between seven and nine o'clock, though the system instead highlights a corresponding evening window if recent activity shows more boarding happening in the evening than the morning. This chart is built entirely from the system's actual recorded ticket timestamps for whichever period is selected, not from a predicted or simulated pattern.

**Passenger Revenue.**
This is the revenue counterpart to the chart described above, using the same hour/day/month/year period selector and the same underlying ticket records, grouped by the clock hour (or day, month, or year) in which each fare was recorded. When the currently selected period has no recorded ticket activity at all, the chart displays a message indicating that no revenue data was detected for that cycle, rather than showing an empty or misleading chart; this message is tied specifically to whichever period the administrator currently has selected, not permanently fixed to "today."

**Peak Passenger Starts.**
Separately from the two charts above, a summary panel identifies the single busiest point in whichever time period is currently selected — the specific busiest hour, day, month, or year — together with the number of tickets recorded during that specific peak, and the pickup locations that contributed most to it during that same window. If no pickup-location data is available for that particular peak period, the panel displays a message to that effect rather than an empty or misleading list. These figures are calculated directly from the system's actual ticket records, identifying the genuinely busiest recorded period rather than an assumed or typical one.

**Live Feed and Live Synchronization Status.**
Several panels throughout the analytics dashboard and its four report-category tabs display a "LIVE" or "LIVE FEED" indicator. It is important to describe precisely what this represents: on the analytics dashboard and all four report-category tabs covered above, these indicators are a fixed visual design element — they do not represent a confirmed, continuously active connection to the server, and the figures behind them do not refresh automatically on their own. The data shown across these views is retrieved once when the page is opened, and again only when the administrator changes a filter; nothing on these particular views updates in real time on its own. This is a meaningful distinction from a separate part of the Reports section, the Daily Operations view, which does maintain a genuine, continuously active connection and does refresh automatically as new activity occurs — but that view draws from an entirely different source of operational data than the ticket-sales analytics described throughout the rest of this documentation, so its live behavior should not be assumed to extend to the figures discussed above. The route-delay sentiment indicator mentioned elsewhere on the page is likewise a fixed status rather than one that varies based on live conditions — see the next section.

**AI Insight.**
A short written insight accompanies the charts described above, describing the identified peak passenger-boarding window and recommending that bus capacity be aligned with it, in the same style of phrasing used in the forecast line described earlier. As with that forecast line, this insight is produced by straightforward, deterministic logic rather than any external artificial-intelligence service — it reads the system's identified peak time window, the associated corridor-name phrase chosen by the same morning-versus-evening comparison described earlier, and a simple recommendation for additional buses that scales only with how many tickets were recorded in the single busiest hour. The route-delay sentiment referenced in this same insight is not calculated from any actual recorded delay, congestion, or passenger-feedback data — it is a fixed status that always reads as stable, regardless of real operating conditions, and should not be read as a live operational signal.

**Report Data Accuracy and Limitations.**
Several limitations are worth stating plainly for an accurate account of this module's current state. First, every revenue figure throughout this module reflects fares assigned to issued tickets, not independently confirmed payments, since the system has no separate payment-confirmation step. Second, a refund-tracking feature referenced in the system's generated insight text is not currently functional in practice — the underlying condition it checks for can never actually be met by the way ticket records are created, so this part of the insight will never appear, by the system's current design, regardless of whether refunds have genuinely occurred. Third, the "Tomorrow Projection" figure and the written forecast and insight text are produced by two or three distinct, independently calculated estimates rather than one single figure reused throughout the page, and all are deterministic calculations rather than outputs of any trained predictive model, despite the "AI" language used to describe them. Fourth, the data-export tool's date range operates independently from the page's main filter controls and does not currently support narrowing an export to a specific bus or route. Fifth, the "LIVE" indicators across the main analytics views are decorative rather than functional, as described above. Finally, on certain kinds of backend unavailability, the dashboard quietly falls back to showing every figure as zero without displaying a visible error message to the administrator — meaning a genuinely empty reporting period and a backend-connectivity failure can, in this specific circumstance, look identical on screen, which is worth being aware of when interpreting an unexpectedly empty report.

**Testing and Verification.**
This documentation was produced entirely through direct inspection of the system's source code. No live browser testing of this module was performed, no automated or service-level tests were run, and no figures were independently verified against the live, deployed system. Every statement above should be understood as a description of what the implemented code is written to do, not as a confirmed observation of the module running in practice.

## Documentation Summary

The Reports & Data Analytics module gives the administrator a single, consolidated place to review the company's ticketing performance — overall and day-to-day revenue and ridership, category-specific breakdowns across passengers, attendants, buses, and routes, and a set of system-generated forecasts and recommendations intended to support near-term planning decisions. Most of what the module presents is calculated directly and transparently from the system's actual ticket records. A smaller set of its more forward-looking features — the day-ahead revenue projection, the month-end outlook, and the narrative insight accompanying them — are the product of straightforward, rule-based calculations rather than genuine artificial-intelligence forecasting, and the module's "live" indicators on its main analytics views are a visual convention rather than a continuously synchronized connection. None of this diminishes the module's practical value as a reporting and planning tool, but an accurate account of the system — the purpose of this documentation — requires describing these specific points precisely rather than allowing the interface's own labeling to stand without clarification.
